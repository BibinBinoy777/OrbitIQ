# OrbitIQ intended use and scientific limits

OrbitIQ supports satellite exploration, inspection of archived orbital inputs,
historical comparisons and clearly identified backend calculations. It is not a
validated operational orbit-determination, navigation, conjunction assessment or
collision-avoidance service.

Positions and ground tracks are calculated, not directly observed. Independent
observation-based prediction validation remains **BLOCKED**, with no quantified
orbital prediction uncertainty. Published numerical-reference agreement tests
implementation behavior for selected cases; it does not establish universal
real-world position accuracy or guaranteed future locations. A recent retrieval
does not refresh an old element epoch. Frame, units, epochs, retrieval/evaluation
times, source identity and EOP/leap limitations remain material to interpretation.

Historical element differences alone do not prove a physical maneuver, anomaly
or collision risk. Missing data or scientific resources must produce explicit
errors, not invented coordinates. The Phase 6.1.1 geodetic precision correction
and approximately 1.317 cm reference-residual explanation remain unchanged;
ellipsoid conversion precision is not satellite prediction accuracy.

Saved investigations retain supported input parameters and exact archive
references. They do not retain or certify the original numerical result. Local
saves belong to a browser/origin, are plaintext and have no cloud synchronization.
Share links are parameter-only read-only reconstructions, require matching source
records and have no secrecy, expiry or revocation guarantee.

Public hosting and a polished globe do not change these limitations. Any future
accuracy claims need an independently identified measurement/reference program,
matched time/frame conventions and measured uncertainty appropriate to the claim.
